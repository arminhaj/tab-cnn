# Related Work

This project is grounded in prior work on automatic music transcription and, more specifically, guitar-specific transcription. The immediate starting point is the TabCNN line of work, which formulates tablature estimation as per-string classification over fret states using convolutional processing of local spectral context. That framing is well suited to guitar because it explicitly models the instrument's six-string structure and treats fingering prediction as more than a generic pitch-recognition problem.

The broader literature on automatic music transcription has shown that time-frequency representations such as constant-Q transforms and mel spectrograms are effective inputs for deep neural networks. CNNs are especially useful because local spectral templates often capture note onsets, harmonics, and timbral cues that correspond to fretting behavior. In guitar transcription, however, purely local evidence can be ambiguous: sustained notes and expressive transitions unfold across multiple frames, and neighboring frames are not independent.

That observation motivates the CRNN extension evaluated here. Recurrent layers have been widely used in sequence modeling to capture short-term temporal dependencies. In this setting, the hope is that a recurrent layer will help stabilize note tracking and improve transcription quality when local acoustic evidence is weak or noisy. The contribution of this project is therefore empirical rather than task-defining: it reproduces the original system, modernizes the training pipeline, and evaluates whether a recurrent extension yields meaningful gains on guitarist-held-out GuitarSet evaluation.
