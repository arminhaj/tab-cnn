# Results

The results are most informative when viewed as a progression rather than as a single terminal comparison. In the earliest failed recurrent runs, performance had effectively collapsed: pitch F1 was approximately 0.15 and tablature F1 was approximately 0.13. These numbers are not best interpreted as evidence against recurrent modeling itself. Instead, they reflect an optimization regime in which the model over-predicted the dominant non-sounding class and produced unrealistically sparse outputs. This distinction is important because it means the early low scores revealed a training pathology before they revealed anything substantive about the representational value of recurrence.

Once that collapse was mitigated, the recurrent approach became much more competitive. The larger bidirectional-GRU variant reached pitch precision 0.8910, pitch recall 0.7759, pitch F1 0.8289, tablature precision 0.7925, tablature recall 0.6954, and tablature F1 0.7403. This was a meaningful intermediate result because it showed that temporal modeling could produce strong transcription quality once the most severe optimization issues had been addressed. At the same time, these gains were not so decisive on the stricter tablature metrics that the larger recurrent architecture could be accepted uncritically as the best design.

The final stabilized comparison between the CNN and the simplified CRNN reveals a more nuanced tradeoff. The CNN achieved pitch precision 0.9077, pitch recall 0.7480, pitch F1 0.8191, tablature precision 0.8203, tablature recall 0.6841, tablature F1 0.7452, and tablature disambiguation rate 0.9040. The simplified CRNN achieved pitch precision 0.8348, pitch recall 0.8499, pitch F1 0.8416, tablature precision 0.7275, tablature recall 0.7622, tablature F1 0.7437, and tablature disambiguation rate 0.8718. The recurrent model therefore achieves the strongest pitch F1 and clearly better recall, while the convolutional baseline remains slightly stronger on exact tablature F1 and more noticeably stronger on disambiguation rate.

This pattern supports a consistent interpretation. Temporal recurrence helps the model maintain note continuity and recover sounding events more aggressively, which explains the recall improvement and the higher pitch-level F1. However, exact string-fret identification remains more difficult. The CNN appears to preserve a sharper local decision boundary for fingering assignments, whereas the CRNN trades some of that local precision for smoother temporal behavior. In other words, the recurrent model improves the easier component of the task, recovering the correct sounding notes, more than it improves the harder component, recovering the precise fretboard position.

Taken together, the results suggest that the project succeeded along two dimensions. First, it established that the apparent early failure of the recurrent approach was not fundamental but optimization-related. Second, it showed that once training was stabilized, the architectural comparison reduced to a meaningful tradeoff rather than a clear winner. If the objective is pitch-level transcription, the recurrent model is preferable. If the objective is exact tablature fidelity, the CNN remains highly competitive and may still be the more favorable operating point.
